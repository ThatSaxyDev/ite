# Chapter 8: The Beach

The beach was in Badagry, a small coastal town about two hours from Lagos. It wasn't the most beautiful beach in Nigeria — the water was brownish, the sand littered with debris, the facilities basic. But it was quiet, uncrowded, and most importantly, it was a break from the chaos of the city.

Jed and Adaeze arrived in the late afternoon, after a long drive through the dusty roads of Ogun State. They checked into a small guesthouse — nothing fancy, just a room with a bed and a bathroom — and walked down to the beach.

The sun was setting, casting the sky in shades of orange and pink. The waves lapped gently at the shore, their sound a soothing contrast to the constant noise of Lagos.

"It's beautiful," Adaeze said, her voice soft with wonder. "I forgot places like this existed."

Jed took her hand. "I forgot too. I've been so focused on work that I forgot to look around."

She turned to him, her eyes searching his face. "Jed, I need to tell you something."

He felt a sudden spike of anxiety. "What is it?"

"I'm pregnant."

The words hung in the air, impossible, terrifying, miraculous. Jed stared at her, unable to speak.

"Are you — are you sure?"

She nodded, a small smile playing at her lips. "I did a test last week. I'm three months along."

Three months. She had known for three months and hadn't told him. He wanted to be angry, to feel betrayed. But all he felt was a overwhelming sense of joy — and fear.

"A baby," he said softly. "We're going to have a baby."

"Are you happy?" she asked, her voice hesitant.

He pulled her into his arms, holding her tightly. "Yes," he whispered. "I'm scared, but I'm happy."

---

They sat on the beach as the stars came out, talking about the future. It was a conversation they had postponed for months, each of them afraid to bring up the topic of marriage and family.

"We should get married," Jed said. "Before the baby comes. I want our child to have a proper family."

Adaeze smiled. "I was hoping you'd say that."

"There's only one problem — I don't have a ring."

She laughed. "You don't need a ring. You just need you."

He kissed her, feeling the weight of the commitment he was making. He was going to be a husband. He was going to be a father. The responsibilities were enormous, terrifying, but also exhilarating.

"What about the business?" Adaeze asked. "With the baby, and marriage — will you be able to manage?"

"I'll manage," Jed said. "I'll have to. But I'll also need your help."

"You'll have it," she said. "Always."

---

They got married three months later, in a small ceremony at the registry office in Lagos. It was a modest affair — just their families and a few close friends. Jed's mother came from the village, resplendent in her traditional attire, tears streaming down her face as she watched her son exchange vows.

"I am so proud of you," she whispered, holding him tightly after the ceremony. "You have made it."

"Not yet, Mama," Jed said. "But I'm getting there."

Bola was the best man, crying openly as he delivered his speech. Chidi was there too, looking awkward in his suit but smiling nonetheless. Tayo had brought his mother, who kept commenting on how handsome the couple looked.

And Adaeze — Adaeze was radiant, her belly beginning to show, her eyes bright with happiness.
